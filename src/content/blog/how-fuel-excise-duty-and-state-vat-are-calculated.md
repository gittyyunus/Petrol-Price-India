Last month my brother-in-law messaged our family group asking why petrol in his city was ₹8 more expensive than mine, even though we live barely 200 km apart. Everyone had a theory — "dealer commission," "transport cost," "your city has better roads so it costs more" (that one made no sense, but okay). Nobody actually knew the real answer.

So I sat down one weekend with a fuel bill, a calculator, and way too many open tabs on my laptop, and actually worked out how the pricing breaks down. Turns out it's not complicated once you see the pieces laid out — it's just that nobody ever shows you the pieces.

I'm sharing exactly what I found, including the part where I got my own state's VAT calculation wrong the first time.

## Why petrol and diesel don't follow GST

Here's the thing that trips up most people, including me until I looked it up properly: petrol and diesel are **not** under GST. When GST rolled out in 2017, fuel was deliberately kept outside it. So instead of one uniform tax, you've got a two-layer system:

1. **Central Excise Duty** — fixed by the central government, same across the country
2. **State VAT** — set individually by each state government, and this is why prices swing so much from state to state

That second point is the whole answer to my brother-in-law's question. His state simply charges a higher VAT percentage than mine.

## The four ingredients in your fuel price

Every litre of petrol or diesel you buy is built from four layers, stacked one on top of the other:

- **Base price** — what the oil company actually pays for crude, refining, and transporting it to the pump
- **Central excise duty** — a flat per-litre amount added by the centre
- **Dealer commission** — what the petrol pump owner earns per litre
- **State VAT** — a percentage charged on top of everything above it

That last point is the one people usually get wrong, including me the first time I tried this. VAT isn't just a percentage of the base price. It's charged on the base price **plus** excise duty **plus** dealer commission, all added together first. So VAT is effectively taxed on tax — it's a compounding effect, not a flat add-on.

## A real calculation, step by step

Let's actually run the numbers instead of just talking about them. I'll use rough, realistic figures — the exact excise and VAT rates do get revised through the year (they've moved around more than usual in 2026 because of global crude price swings), so treat these as illustrative rather than today's exact figure. Always check the current rate on the Petroleum Planning & Analysis Cell (PPAC) website before quoting numbers anywhere official.

Say the numbers for one litre of petrol look like this:

1. Base price (crude cost + refining + freight): **₹56.00**
2. Central excise duty: **₹11.90**
3. Dealer commission: **₹3.90**
4. Subtotal before VAT: 56.00 + 11.90 + 3.90 = **₹71.80**
5. State VAT (say the state charges 25%): 25% of 71.80 = **₹17.95**
6. **Final retail price: 71.80 + 17.95 = ₹89.75 per litre**

Now here's the part that surprised me. If a neighbouring state charges 30% VAT instead of 25%, on that same ₹71.80 subtotal:

- VAT = 30% of 71.80 = **₹21.54**
- Final price = 71.80 + 21.54 = **₹93.34**

That's a ₹3.59 difference per litre, purely from a 5-percentage-point VAT gap, even though the base price and excise duty were identical in both states. Multiply that across a 40-litre tank and you're paying roughly ₹144 more just for filling up on the wrong side of a state border. This is exactly why people near state borders (Haryana-Delhi, or Maharashtra-Karnataka border towns) sometimes drive an extra kilometre to fill up in the cheaper state.

## Where I went wrong the first time

When I first tried this calculation, I applied VAT only to the base price, not to the base price plus excise plus commission. My number came out way lower than the actual pump price, and I genuinely thought the petrol pump was overcharging me.

Turns out that's the whole trick of how this tax structure works — VAT is calculated on an amount that already includes another tax. Tax experts call this "cascading," and it's one of the reasons fuel taxation contributes so much more to the final price than most people expect. Roughly half of what you pay at the pump, sometimes more, is tax in one form or another.

## How to check this for your own city

You don't need to trust my numbers. Here's how I verify it now whenever fuel prices change:

**Step 1** — Open the PPAC website (ppac.gov.in) or your oil marketing company's app (Indian Oil, HP, or BPCL all have daily price breakup pages). They publish the exact base price, excise, dealer commission, and VAT for major cities.

**Step 2** — Note the four components separately. Don't just look at the final price.

**Step 3** — Add base price + excise + dealer commission to get the pre-VAT subtotal.

**Step 4** — Apply your state's VAT percentage (this is published on your state's commercial tax department website, or you can just back-calculate it from the PPAC breakup).

**Step 5** — Compare your calculated number to the actual pump price. If they match within a few paise, you've got it right. If they're way off, you've probably applied VAT to the wrong base amount — that was my mistake.

## Common mistakes people make with this

- **Assuming VAT is a flat national rate.** It isn't. Every state sets its own, and some states also add a small additional cess on top.
- **Applying VAT only to the base fuel cost.** As I found out the hard way, it applies to the base cost plus excise duty plus dealer commission.
- **Comparing excise duty changes directly to pump price changes.** When the centre cuts excise duty, states don't have to pass on proportional VAT savings, so a ₹2 excise cut doesn't always mean a full ₹2 drop at the pump — sometimes it's more, sometimes less, because VAT recalculates on the new lower subtotal.
- **Forgetting diesel and petrol have different excise rates.** They're not taxed at the same per-litre rate — today's gap is a few rupees per litre in diesel's favour — so you can't use petrol numbers to estimate diesel prices. The full story of why that differential exists is in [why diesel is cheaper than petrol](/blog/why-diesel-is-cheaper-than-petrol/).
- **Ignoring that rates change through the year.** Both the centre and states revise their rates periodically, especially when global crude prices move sharply, so a number that was accurate three months ago might already be outdated.

## Final thoughts

Once you actually break down the four layers and run one real calculation, the whole "why does fuel cost so much" mystery stops feeling mysterious. It's not some hidden conspiracy at the pump — it's a fairly transparent structure, it's just that nobody hands you the breakup unless you go looking for it.

Next time someone in your family group chat asks why fuel is cheaper one state over, you'll have an actual answer instead of a guess about road quality.
