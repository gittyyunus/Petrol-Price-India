A cab driver I know switched his sedan to Autogas a couple of years back, mostly chasing the lower running cost. What surprised him wasn't the savings — those were real — it was that Autogas prices moved almost daily, the same way petrol does. He'd assumed, like a lot of people do, that any "alternative fuel" runs on some simpler, flatter pricing system. It doesn't.

That mix-up is common, so I dug into how Autogas pricing is actually built — checking PPAC data and a few distributor pricing pages — and it turns out the structure is genuinely closer to petrol's than to CNG's, even though all three get lumped together as "alternative fuels."

## First, a quick clarification worth getting right

Autogas is the automotive version of LPG — same base product (a propane-butane mix) as the cylinder in your kitchen, but taxed and priced completely differently once it's sold as vehicle fuel rather than a subsidized domestic cooking cylinder. Don't confuse the two — your household LPG cylinder runs under a separate GST-based, partly subsidized system. Autogas, sold at a pump for vehicles, does not.

## The four layers that build an Autogas price

**1. Import Parity Price (IPP).** India imports a large share of the propane and butane that goes into Autogas, so the starting cost is tied to international LPG benchmarks, ocean freight, insurance, and — critically — the rupee-to-dollar exchange rate. When the rupee weakens, this layer gets more expensive even if nothing else changes. This is the same mechanism petrol and diesel pricing leans on, which is part of why Autogas tends to move in a similar rhythm to petrol.

**2. Central excise duty.** Autogas carries a per-litre excise duty from the central government, similar in structure to petrol's flat per-litre excise (though not necessarily the same rate — petrol's excise tends to run higher). This is a fixed amount added regardless of where in India you're filling up.

**3. Dealer/OMC margin.** The oil marketing company and the retail dealer both take a cut, covering distribution, storage, and running the outlet. This is a relatively small layer compared to the base cost, but it's a real one and it does vary somewhat by dealer.

**4. State VAT.** Same story as petrol and diesel — each state sets its own VAT rate on Autogas, and this is layered on top of the base price, excise, and dealer margin combined, not just on the raw fuel cost. That compounding is exactly why VAT differences between states create bigger final-price gaps than the VAT percentage alone would suggest.

## A worked example

Say the import parity cost for a litre of Autogas comes to around ₹38. Add a central excise duty of roughly ₹10, and a dealer margin of ₹2, and you're at a pre-VAT subtotal of about ₹50.

Apply an 18% state VAT and the final price comes to roughly ₹59. In a neighboring state charging 24% VAT on that same ₹50 subtotal, the price works out closer to ₹62.

That gap — a few rupees per litre from VAT alone — is a big part of why Autogas prices can differ noticeably even between cities that aren't far apart. Treat these figures as illustrative; actual import costs and VAT rates shift often enough that you're better off checking current numbers directly — a page like [Autogas price by city in India](/autogas-price-in-india/) is a faster way to compare live rates than trying to reconstruct the math from memory.

## How Autogas pricing actually differs from CNG's

This is where a lot of people (myself included, at first) get the comparison wrong. Both Autogas and CNG are taxed through central excise plus state VAT — neither one is GST-based or somehow "tax-light." The real differences are in mechanism, not in whether tax applies:

- **Autogas's excise is a flat per-litre amount**, closer to how petrol's excise works. **CNG's excise is percentage-based**, applied differently.
- **Autogas pricing leans heavily on Import Parity Price**, since a large share of it is imported. **CNG pricing leans more on the split between cheaper domestic (APM) gas and costlier imported LNG**, which each city's gas distributor manages differently.
- **Autogas doesn't have the same centralized daily revision mechanism as petrol**, but it moves fairly often based on import costs. **CNG pricing is set independently by each licensed city gas distributor**, which is part of why CNG prices can vary more sharply between cities than Autogas prices do.

So if you're cross-shopping the two, don't assume one is "the taxed one" and the other isn't — both carry real tax layers, they're just built slightly differently.

## Checking this for yourself

**Step 1** — Look up your city's current Autogas price through your state OMC's retail pricing page or a fuel-price tracking site that updates daily.

**Step 2** — Cross-check the VAT component against PPAC (ppac.gov.in), which lists state-wise VAT rates on petroleum products.

**Step 3** — If you're comparing prices across two states, separate out how much of the gap is VAT versus how much might be transport/logistics cost to that region — they're not the same thing, and treating a transport-cost gap as "just tax" will throw off your math.

**Step 4** — Don't assume last week's number holds. Since IPP is tied to global LPG prices and the exchange rate, Autogas can shift even without any tax change at all.

## Common mistakes people make with Autogas pricing

**Assuming Autogas and household LPG cylinders are priced the same way.** They're not — cylinder LPG runs under GST with partial subsidy for many households; Autogas at the pump does not get that treatment.

**Assuming Autogas skips taxation the way people wrongly assume CNG does.** It doesn't. Excise duty and state VAT both apply, in a structure genuinely close to petrol's.

**Blaming state VAT for every price gap.** Import cost and exchange-rate movements can shift the base price too — VAT is one lever, not the only one.

**Not accounting for currency swings.** Since a meaningful share of the cost traces back to imported LPG, a weaker rupee can push prices up even in a week with no domestic tax changes at all.

**Comparing Autogas to CNG like they're priced identically.** They both involve tax, but the underlying cost structures — import-heavy for Autogas, domestic/imported-gas-mix for CNG — are different enough that a straight price comparison without understanding why can be misleading.

## Final thoughts

Autogas isn't some tax-free workaround, and it isn't priced like CNG either — it runs closer to petrol's playbook: import-driven base cost, flat excise duty, dealer margin, and state VAT stacked on top, with the rupee's strength as a quiet but real factor in the background.

Once you know where the four pieces come from, a price swing stops feeling random. It's either the rupee moved, global LPG prices shifted, or your state adjusted VAT — and now you know which question to actually ask instead of just wondering why the pump price changed again.
