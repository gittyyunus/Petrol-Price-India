A colleague of mine spent three weeks last month choosing between the petrol and diesel versions of the same mid-size SUV. The dealer told him diesel is "always cheaper in the long run." His father, who owned diesel cars through the 2010s, told him petrol is safer after all the Delhi pollution rules. His Excel sheet had fourteen rows, none of which matched what I could reconstruct in five.

So I did what I always do — pulled today's pump prices, checked the actual on-road price gap between the two variants, found honest owner-reported mileage for both, and added up five years of fuel, service, insurance and resale. The answer was neither "always petrol" nor "always diesel." It was a threshold — a specific annual kilometre number below which diesel is a trap, and above which it quietly pays you back. And that threshold moves depending on which segment of car you're buying.

Here's the full working, the Delhi-NCR rules as they stand in 2026, and a plain verdict for each type of buyer.

**The short answer:** at Delhi rates on 13 October 2026 — petrol ₹102.12 a litre, diesel ₹95.20 — a real-world petrol hatch covering 18 km/l costs ₹5.67 a kilometre, a diesel one covering 22 km/l costs ₹4.33. Diesel saves about ₹1.34 every kilometre, or ₹20,000 a year at 15,000 km. Sounds decisive — until you notice the diesel variant costs ₹1.0–1.2 lakh more to buy, which means breakeven arrives around 75,000–90,000 kilometres: five to six years of driving, right at or past the point many people sell. In a hatchback at average mileage, **diesel is a coin toss that usually lands petrol-side**. In a mid-size SUV — where diesel real-world efficiency is around 16 km/l against petrol's 12, the saving jumps to ₹2.56 a kilometre, and the same ₹1.5 lakh premium pays back in roughly three years — **diesel wins clearly if you keep the car five years or more**. Below 12,000 km a year, petrol. Above 20,000–25,000 km a year, diesel — or CNG, which is cheaper per kilometre than both. The Delhi-NCR buyer has one extra filter: only BS6 diesel, because BS-IV and older diesels now face GRAP driving restrictions while BS6 is exempt.

## Why this question got harder between 2015 and 2026

Ten years ago the answer was arithmetic. Diesel was ₹20–25 cheaper than petrol per litre in most states, diesel engines returned 25–30% more kilometres per unit of fuel, and the combination was so one-sided that diesel crossed half of all passenger car sales. Buy diesel, drive enough, save.

Four things broke that shortcut:

- **The price gap collapsed.** In Delhi today the difference is ₹6.92 a litre, not ₹25. (It varies wildly by state — more on that below, because the gap in Mumbai and Kolkata is still ₹13+, which is a story of state VAT, not crude oil. I've layered that whole stack in [how fuel excise duty and state VAT are calculated](/blog/how-fuel-excise-duty-and-state-vat-are-calculated/).)
- **BS6 made diesel cars expensive.** BS6 Phase 2, effective April 2023, forces real-driving-emissions compliance: diesel particulate filters, heavier aftertreatment, in some engines urea dosing. That hardware added ₹50,000–₹1,00,000 to diesel variant prices and shrank the efficiency gap petrol was always losing.
- **The rules turned hostile to old diesels**, particularly in Delhi-NCR (covered in its own section below).
- **Rivals ate the middle.** Factory CNG now does city running cheaper than diesel, and strong hybrids return 20–25 km/l without any of diesel's complications.

The market's verdict is visible in registration data. Of the 47.05 lakh passenger vehicles sold in FY2026, petrol took 47.48%, CNG 21.98%, **diesel 18.08%**, hybrids 8.21% and EVs 4.25%. Diesel's share has even ticked back up — FADA registration data puts it at 19.39% in the early months of FY2027, and September 2026 registrations showed diesel at 18.5%, up year-on-year. But look at *who* is buying it: Mahindra alone accounts for about 56.6% of all diesel cars registered in India in 2026, followed by Hyundai and Kia — that is, SUV makers. Diesel didn't die. It became an SUV fuel, chosen deliberately by people who tow, climb, load seven seats and drive long distances.

Which is exactly why "which is better?" now has a segment-specific answer.

## The only two numbers that actually decide this

Every petrol-versus-diesel debate collapses into two calculations. Ignore per-litre pump prices — a litre of petrol and a litre of diesel are different units carrying different energy and returning different mileage.

**Number 1: cost per kilometre = fuel price ÷ real-world mileage.**

**Number 2: breakeven distance = diesel variant premium ÷ per-kilometre saving.**

Everything else — torque, resale, rules — is a tiebreaker between two answers those numbers already gave you.

### The hatchback math at today's Delhi rates

Using owner-reported city mileage (not ARAI figures, which run 15–25% optimistic — I've explained why in [my fuel pump mileage tests piece](/blog/why-my-motorcycle-mileage-dropped-fuel-pump-tests/)):

| | Petrol hatchback | Diesel hatchback |
|---|---|---|
| Pump price (Delhi, 13 Oct 2026) | ₹102.12 / litre | ₹95.20 / litre |
| Real-world mileage | ~18 km / litre | ~22 km / litre |
| **Cost per kilometre** | **₹5.67** | **₹4.33** |
| 15,000 km every year | ₹85,050 | ₹64,950 |
| **Annual saving on diesel** | — | **₹20,100** |

Now the price of admission: the diesel variant of a typical hatchback costs ₹1.0–1.2 lakh more on-road.

₹1,10,000 ÷ ₹1.34 per km = **~82,000 km** to break even.

At 15,000 km a year that is **5.5 years**. At 10,000 km a year, it is **over eight years** — well past the point most hatchback owners sell. Diesel does eventually become free money; the problem is that "eventually" is doing a lot of heavy lifting in that sentence.

### Your state matters more than you think

Delhi's ₹6.92 gap is actually one of the *smallest* in the country, because state VAT on diesel is structured differently from petrol in every state. Same hatchback, same 18/22 km/l, four metros on the same day:

| City | Petrol ₹/L | Diesel ₹/L | Price gap | Petrol ₹/km | Diesel ₹/km | Diesel saves |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Delhi | 102.12 | 95.20 | ₹6.92 | ₹5.67 | ₹4.33 | ₹1.34 / km |
| Mumbai | 111.21 | 97.83 | ₹13.38 | ₹6.18 | ₹4.45 | ₹1.73 / km |
| Chennai | 107.77 | 99.55 | ₹8.22 | ₹5.99 | ₹4.53 | ₹1.46 / km |
| Kolkata | 113.51 | 99.82 | ₹13.69 | ₹6.31 | ₹4.54 | ₹1.77 / km |

In Kolkata and Mumbai, diesel's annual saving at 15,000 km climbs to ₹26,000–₹26,500 — breakeven drops to about four years, and the same hatchback that was a coin toss in Delhi becomes a genuine diesel case. Never calculate with another city's numbers; check [today's petrol price](/petrol-price-in-india/) and [diesel price for your own city](/diesel-price-in-india/) first. I track how these state-level differences stack up in my [state-wise petrol price ranking for 2026](/blog/state-wise-petrol-price-ranking-2026/).

## The SUV and sedan maths: where diesel actually earns its keep

The reason diesel survives at 18–19% market share is that the same ₹6.92 gap hits a much bigger hole in a bigger car. A petrol mid-size SUV does about 12 km/l in real city driving; the diesel does 15–16.

| | Petrol SUV | Diesel SUV |
|---|---|---|
| Cost per kilometre (12 vs 16 km/l) | ₹8.51 | ₹5.95 |
| 18,000 km every year | ₹1,53,180 | ₹1,07,100 |
| Annual saving | — | **₹46,080** |
| Typical diesel premium | — | ₹1,50,000 |
| **Breakeven** | — | **~58,600 km ≈ 3.25 years** |

Three and a half years to breakeven on a car most people keep for six or seven — after that, every kilometre is ₹2.56 in your pocket. A sedan sits between the two cases: roughly ₹1.6–1.8 saved per kilometre, breakeven around four years at 15,000 km annually.

This is the whole reason the dealership floor looks the way it does: the SUV buyer is diesel's best customer because the maths genuinely favours them.

## Five years, total cost: both cases worked out

Fuel saving alone lies. Here's the honest five-year addition including the premium and a realistic maintenance allowance (diesel services cost more — details in the next section):

**Hatchback, 15,000 km/year (75,000 km over five years):**

| | Petrol | Diesel |
|---|---|---|
| Fuel bill | ₹4,25,250 | ₹3,24,750 |
| Upfront variant premium | — | +₹1,10,000 |
| Extra servicing/ownership cost | — | +₹15,000 |
| **Five-year total** | **₹4,25,250** | **₹4,49,750** |

Diesel ends roughly **₹25,000 behind** — inside the error bar of a coin toss, with you carrying the risk of an expensive repair on a more complex engine.

**Mid-size SUV, 18,000 km/year (90,000 km over five years):**

| | Petrol | Diesel |
|---|---|---|
| Fuel bill | ₹7,65,900 | ₹5,35,500 |
| Upfront variant premium | — | +₹1,50,000 |
| Extra servicing/ownership cost | — | +₹20,000 |
| **Five-year total** | **₹7,65,900** | **₹7,05,500** |

Diesel finishes about **₹60,000 ahead** — and the gap keeps widening after year five. Push the SUV to 25,000 km a year and diesel wins by well over a lakh.

The pattern is consistent: **the higher your annual distance and the bigger the car, the more often diesel wins.** My earlier [petrol vs diesel vs CNG running-costs breakdown](/blog/petrol-vs-diesel-vs-cng-running-costs/) reached the same conclusion from three months of real fuel bills, and the numbers still hold at October 2026 rates.

## What diesel costs that petrol doesn't

The per-kilometre table is the marketing. This is the small print:

- **Diesel particulate filter (DPF) care.** Modern diesels burn soot in a filter that has to periodically "regenerate" at sustained speeds — typically a 30–40 minute highway run or a long, steady stretch. A diesel driven exclusively on short city trips never regenerates properly: the soot loads up, you get a warning light, limp mode, and in the bad case a dealer-forced regeneration or a replacement that can run ₹40,000–₹80,000. Rule of thumb: **if your daily drive is under 15 km, mostly in traffic, a diesel is the wrong tool.** Once or twice a month, take it out for a proper run.
- **Urea dosing on select engines.** A few BS6 diesels use SCR and sip an additive (AdBlue/DEF) you top up every few thousand kilometres; many others — Maruti's 1.5 DDiS, most Tata and Mahindra diesels — rely on DPF alone. Check your manual before budgeting; where it applies, high-mileage drivers should allow ₹3,000–₹8,000 a year.
- **Service bills run 15–25% higher.** Bigger oil capacity, fuel filters that need changing more often, more complex diagnostics. Over 75,000 km I've typically seen ₹40,000–₹55,000 for diesel against ₹30,000–₹40,000 for petrol — nothing catastrophic, just a steady drag on your savings.
- **Insurance costs a little more**, because the insured value of the costlier variant is higher. Expect a few percentage points on the own-damage premium, every year, for as long as you own the car.
- **Noise and vibration.** Diesel idle clatter and low-speed vibration are real. Refinement has improved enormously — modern diesels are nothing like a 2010 taxi engine — but a petrol is still smoother and quieter, especially from cold. If refinement is why you buy a premium car, petrol is the better fit.

## And what petrol costs that diesel doesn't

Honesty cuts both ways:

- **Lower torque down low means smaller, cheaper engines.** A 1.2-litre petrol handles a hatchback fine; equivalent diesel performance needs a turbo, beefier clutch, and stronger driveline — that's part of the ₹1 lakh premium you already paid.
- **The E20 transition.** India's petrol is moving to 20% ethanol; new petrol cars are E20-ready from the factory, but older pre-E20 cars can see slight mileage drops and seal wear on the new blend. I covered the compatibility question in [what E20 petrol means for your car](/blog/what-is-e20-petrol/). Diesel sits outside this entirely — one genuine advantage it keeps.
- **No DPF anxiety.** Petrol has no regeneration cycle, no soot warnings, no "you must drive it on the highway or it'll clog" condition. For pure-city lives, petrol's simplicity is worth money even when the maths says otherwise.
- **Resale breadth.** Petrol cars sell anywhere, to anyone, without questions about emission norms or city rules — a point worth real rupees, covered next.

## Resale: the number that changes the whole equation

Any breakeven calculation assumes you keep the car until the savings arrive. Resale decides whether the market lets you.

From the resale listings I've compared over the past year: a three-year-old diesel hatchback typically asks 8–12% below the equivalent petrol, and pre-BS6 diesels in Delhi-NCR can sit 20–30% below — dealers price in the registration risk and the smaller buyer pool. SUVs are the exception: diesel SUVs and pickups hold value well precisely because the new-car market has consolidated diesel into SUVs, and used buyers seeking torque have few factory-fresh alternatives. Mahindra's dominance of the diesel registrations (56.6% share in 2026) is not accidental — it's a resale ecosystem around diesel 4x4s and utility vehicles.

Practical translation: **diesel hatchback resale = weak; diesel sedan = middling; diesel SUV = strong.** Fold this into your breakeven: if you sell at year four and the diesel loses an extra ₹40,000–₹60,000 on the used market, a five-year breakeven never happens at all.

## The Delhi-NCR 10-year rule: what is actually true in 2026

This deserves its own section because it's where most advice online is stale or plain wrong.

The original rule: the NGT banned diesel vehicles older than 10 years (petrol: 15 years) from plying in Delhi-NCR back in 2015, upheld by the Supreme Court in 2018. For a decade that meant every diesel's resale clock effectively stopped at ten.

Then three updates rewrote it:

1. **August 2025** — the Supreme Court stayed coercive action against owners purely on the grounds of a vehicle being 10 years old (diesel) or 15 years old (petrol), while it heard reviews.
2. **December 2025** — the Court modified that relief: **only BS4 and BS6 vehicles are protected regardless of age.** BS-1, BS-2 and BS-3 vehicles (over 14 lakh, 38 lakh and 53 lakh vehicles respectively) are back in scope. A BS4 diesel from 2017 is currently protected; a BS3 diesel from 2015 is not.
3. **GRAP restrictions, updated September 2026** — under the Graded Response Action Plan, **BS-III petrol and BS-IV-or-older diesel cars face plying restrictions in Delhi and the high-density districts of Gurugram, Faridabad, Ghaziabad, Gautam Budh Nagar and Sonipat.** BS6 is exempt. Separately, CAQM's direction to deny fuel to end-of-life vehicles at pumps via number-plate cameras has been staged and paused over enforcement glitches, but the underlying legal direction stands.

What this means when buying diesel in 2026:

- **Buying new or BS6?** Age-based bans are no longer your problem. The 10-year fear that killed diesel resale in NCR has genuinely loosened — for BS6 cars.
- **Buying a used BS4 diesel in NCR?** You're protected from age-based coercion today, but GRAP still restricts your car during pollution episodes, and a future Court order could narrow the relief again. Price that risk.
- **Buying any diesel (or petrol) BS3-and-older in NCR?** Don't. The protection explicitly excludes those vintages.
- **Outside NCR?** These rules don't bind you — but note they're the template other metros keep borrowing from whenever winter smog makes headlines.

## The emissions argument, honestly

Both sides quote emissions like lawyers. The actual data:

| Measure | Petrol | Diesel |
|---|---|---|
| CO2 per kilometre | ~190 g / km | ~165–175 g / km — about **8–13% less** |
| Why | lower thermal efficiency | diesel engines convert 40–45% of fuel energy to motion vs petrol's 30–36% |
| Particulate matter (PM) | low, but GDI engines do emit some | high without a DPF; **BS6 DPF cuts it by 90%+** |
| NOx | well handled by the three-way catalytic converter | historically diesel's weak point; SCR/urea treats it where fitted |
| Urban smog contribution | significant | significant — older diesels disproportionately so |

Diesel genuinely emits less CO2 per kilometre — the efficiency advantage is thermodynamics, not marketing. It also genuinely emitted far worse NOx and particulates for years, which is exactly why the rules turned on it. A properly maintained BS6 diesel with a working DPF is a world away from a 2012 one — which is what the CAQM's own data shows: a BS3 diesel produces over six times the NOx and more than twice the PM of a BS4 car. If clean air is your priority, the choice isn't petrol vs diesel, it's *modern vs old* — any fuel.

## Two rivals nobody mentions in this debate

A petrol-vs-diesel comparison in 2026 that ignores its neighbours is incomplete:

- **CNG.** In every major metro, factory CNG now costs ₹3.62–₹3.95 per kilometre — cheaper than diesel's ₹4.33, with no ₹1 lakh variant premium comparable to the CNG premium you pay back in two to three years. If your city has stations near home and office, the real question isn't petrol vs diesel, it's whether CNG fits your boot space and range needs. The full case is in [why CNG beats petrol for most Indian drivers](/blog/why-cng-is-better-than-petrol/).
- **Strong hybrids.** A petrol hybrid delivering 20–25 km/l beats diesel on efficiency, beats it on refinement, carries none of the DPF risk, and — because road tax and registration are computed on petrol rates in most states — often costs less than the diesel variant too. It's the reason diesel's share, while stable, keeps capping out under 20%.

The honest framing: petrol and diesel are no longer the only two candidates on the ballot.

## Six buyers, six clear answers

| Your profile | Buy | Why |
|---|---|---|
| City commuter, under 12,000 km/year, hatch/sedan | **Petrol** | breakeven never arrives inside your ownership window; simpler, better resale |
| 12,000–18,000 km/year in a hatchback | **Petrol, or CNG if stations are near you** | diesel's 5.5-year breakeven on ₹1.1 lakh is too slow for a car you'll likely sell first |
| 18,000+ km/year, mid-size SUV or 7-seater | **Diesel** | ₹2.56/km saved, ~3-year breakeven, torque for load and gradients |
| 25,000+ km/year — cabs, fleets, long commutes | **Diesel or CNG** | highest annual savings; CNG wins the per-km crown where available |
| Delhi-NCR, keeping the car 6+ years | **BS6 diesel or petrol** | BS6 is exempt from age actions; BS-IV and older face GRAP restrictions |
| Highway-heavy, towing, hilly terrain, loaded trips | **Diesel** | low-end torque, engine braking and 600+ km range that petrol engines match badly |

## Common mistakes people make

- **Comparing pump prices per litre.** ₹102 vs ₹95 means nothing; ₹5.67 vs ₹4.33 per kilometre means everything. Do the division before you argue.
- **Using ARAI mileage in the calculation.** Certified figures are lab numbers. Owner forums (Team-BHP, model-specific Facebook groups) give you the 15–25% lower reality — use those.
- **Buying diesel for 8,000–10,000 km a year because "diesel is cheaper."** It was cheaper per kilometre in 2012. Today it's cheaper per kilometre but dearer per car, and at low annual distances the second number wins every time.
- **City-only diesel driving.** No regular highway runs means no DPF regeneration. This is the single most expensive mistake on this list.
- **Applying Delhi's fuel gap to your city.** Mumbai's gap is ₹13.38; Delhi's is ₹6.92. Always use your own city's rates from our [petrol](/petrol-price-in-india/) and [diesel](/diesel-price-in-india/) pages.
- **Forgetting resale in the maths.** A breakeven that lands after your typical sale year is worth zero. Check used-car listings for your model *before* booking.
- **Skipping the CNG and hybrid check entirely.** Two of the three cheaper options in this article are in the other tabs of the showroom — spend ten minutes there before deciding the 2015 petrol-vs-diesel question.

## Step-by-step: decide for your own case in ten minutes

**Step 1** — Note today's petrol and diesel prices for your city, and the exact on-road price difference between the two variants you're actually choosing between (ask the dealer for the variant-wise sheet, not the ex-showroom rounding).

**Step 2** — Find real-world mileage for both versions of your specific model from owners, not brochures.

**Step 3** — Calculate both cost-per-km figures. The difference is your per-kilometre saving.

**Step 4** — Divide the on-road premium by the saving. That's your breakeven in kilometres. Divide by your realistic annual distance — that's your breakeven in years.

**Step 5** — Compare breakeven years to how long you'll actually keep the car. Under three years: petrol. Three to five: it depends on segment (SUV → diesel, hatch → petrol). Over five: diesel usually wins.

**Step 6** — Run the two filters: (a) is your driving mostly short city trips? If yes, diesel's DPF will fight you — pick petrol or CNG instead. (b) In Delhi-NCR, is the car BS6? If used and not BS6, price in the restriction risk.

**Step 7** — Check resale: search your model on the major used-car platforms, filter by variant and age, and see what diesel is actually fetching versus petrol. Subtract any gap from diesel's projected savings before you decide.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a diesel car worth buying in 2026 in India?

Yes — in the right case. If you drive 18,000+ km a year, especially in an SUV or 7-seater, and keep the car five years or more, diesel returns ₹60,000–₹1,00,000+ over petrol after accounting for the purchase premium. If you drive a hatchback under 15,000 km a year in the city, it isn't: breakeven stretches to 5.5–8 years and resale works against you. Diesel's 18–19% market share in 2026 reflects exactly this — it survived by becoming the high-mileage and SUV choice.

### Which is cheaper to maintain, petrol or diesel?

Petrol, consistently. Diesel services run 15–25% higher (fuel filters, larger oil capacity, more complex diagnostics), insurance costs a little more on the costlier variant, and there are diesel-only risks — DPF service or replacement (₹40,000–₹80,000 in the worst case), urea top-ups on SCR-equipped engines. Against diesel's fuel savings of ₹20,000–₹46,000 a year these are usually outweighed — but they must be in your spreadsheet, not absent from it.

### Do diesel cars still have a future in India?

Yes, but a narrower one. Diesel accounted for 18.08% of passenger vehicles sold in FY2026 and ticked up to 19.39% in early FY2027 — concentrated overwhelmingly in SUVs, with Mahindra alone making up 56.6% of diesel registrations. The era of the diesel hatchback is effectively over; the diesel SUV, backed by torque and highway economy, is secure for the rest of this decade.

### Is diesel banned in Delhi now?

Not categorically. The 10-year age ban's current status: after the Supreme Court's December 2025 modification, **BS4 and BS6 vehicles are protected regardless of age**, while BS3-and-older vehicles remain actionable under the end-of-life regime. Separately, GRAP restrictions bar BS-IV-and-older diesel cars from plying in Delhi and the NCR high-density districts (Gurugram, Faridabad, Ghaziabad, Gautam Budh Nagar, Sonipat) during pollution-response periods. A new or BS6 diesel is legal and unrestricted; an old BS3 one is neither.

### Which gives better mileage, petrol or diesel?

Diesel, always — typically 15–30% more kilometres per equivalent fuel unit because of higher energy density and better thermal efficiency. A petrol hatch at 18 km/l usually has a diesel sibling at 20–22 km/l; a petrol SUV at 12 km/l has a diesel at 15–16 km/l. But mileage is only half the equation — the diesel variant's higher price has to be repaid first, which is why better mileage doesn't automatically mean a better decision.

### Which holds resale value better, petrol or diesel?

Petrol, as a category — it sells anywhere, to anyone, with no questions about emission vintages or city restrictions. Two important exceptions: diesel SUVs and utility vehicles hold value well (the used market's torque-seekers have few alternatives), and in Delhi-NCR the diesel discount is concentrated in pre-BS6 cars. A five-year-old BS6 diesel SUV can outsell a petrol equivalent; a seven-year-old diesel hatch in NCR will not.

## Final thoughts

Petrol vs diesel was never a contest of fuels — it's a contest between two numbers: what the diesel premium costs you today and what the per-kilometre saving gives back over exactly the years you'll own the car. In a hatchback at average city mileage, the two numbers arrive too close together to justify choosing the more complex engine. In a high-mileage SUV, diesel wins with room to spare — and in Mumbai or Kolkata, where the state's fuel tax widens the gap to ₹13 a litre, it wins earlier still.
