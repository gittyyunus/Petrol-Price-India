A few months ago I was about to leave for a long drive and wanted to check that day's fuel rate before heading out. I opened what I thought was a reliable app, saw a number, filled up based on it, and then noticed at the pump that the actual price was almost ₹2 higher than what the app showed. Turned out the app hadn't updated in three days, and I hadn't even checked the "last updated" timestamp because I didn't know these apps could lag that much.

That annoyed me enough to sit down and systematically test every fuel price app and website I could find over the next three weeks. I checked each one against the price board at the pump every morning, compared how fast they updated, and noted which were genuinely useful versus which only looked useful. Here's what I found.

## Why the "last updated" field matters more than the app name

Fuel prices in India refresh at 6 AM every day. But apps don't all pull that update at the same speed — some sync within minutes, some take a day, and a couple I tested were showing prices two or three days old without anyone noticing.

So the single habit that matters more than picking the "best" app is this: always check the "last updated" timestamp before trusting the number. If it reads today, the price is real. If it's old or missing entirely, treat the figure as stale and check elsewhere.

## The apps and sites I actually tested

### The official OMC apps

**Fuel@IOC (Indian Oil)** — This is Indian Oil's official app and the most accurate I tested, because the data comes straight from Indian Oil itself. It shows the daily price for your city and lists its station network. It can be a little slow to load, but it's the reliable baseline.

**My HPCL / HP Pay (Hindustan Petroleum)** — HP's app has expanded well beyond prices and now covers payments, wallet offers, and more. The price-check part is solid, and it's genuinely useful if you already use HPCL stations.

**SmartDrive (Bharat Petroleum)** — BP's app does real-time price checks and station location. It's simple and it works, though it didn't feel like it stood out beyond the others.

I kept Fuel@IOC and HP Pay; SmartDrive didn't earn a home-screen slot.

### Websites

**GoodReturns and MyPetrolPrice** — These aggregator sites pull up-to-date city-wise rates for petrol and diesel. They're handy for quickly comparing a few cities, but the data can lag the official announcement, so verify the timestamp.

**[PetrolPriceIndia](https://petrolpriceindia.com/)** — A browser-based site that lists city-wise petrol and diesel from the same official public data. It's the one I ended up bookmarking because it's fast and works without installing yet another app.

## Why checking two sources is the real trick

The habit that caught the stale app on my opening story: always cross-check against a second source. In practice I just use the official app for the day and glance at one of the websites when I'm comparing multiple cities or planning a trip — two sources, confirmed, before I believe a number enough to drive to a particular pump.

## A practical routine that takes 30 seconds

- **Step 1** — Keep one official app (of the brand you usually use).
- **Step 2** — Keep one website bookmarked for a quick second check.
- **Step 3** — Verify the "last updated" timestamp before every trip.
- **Step 4** — For long drives, check the destination's rates the day before, since state-wise differences (VAT) can make the fuller much cheaper across a border.

## Errors I made while testing

- **Trusting a cached number without checking the timestamp** — the exact mistake that started this whole experiment.
- **Assuming all apps update at the same moment.** They don't; that's the whole problem.
- **Relying on a single source.** One app, one number, no cross-check — that's how the stale app slipped past me.

## Final thoughts

Nobody needs ten fuel apps. The reliable setup is one official app, one site, and — above all — the discipline to check when a number was last updated before you drive anywhere on it. That 30-second habit costs nothing and removes the specific failure that wasted my ₹2 per litre that morning.
