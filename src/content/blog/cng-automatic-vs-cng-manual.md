The salesman had two keys in his hand and a number in his head. "Sir, CNG automatic is only thirty-five thousand more. Mileage is also more — thirty-five point three four, manual is thirty-two point eight five."

He was quoting the brochure correctly. That is what makes the pitch so effective, and so misleading.

I had gone along with a friend who was buying his first car, a Swift, and had already decided on CNG because he drives 1,100 km a month in Pune traffic. His question was narrower: manual or AMT? The salesman answered with a mileage number that made the automatic look like the frugal choice. It isn't. The ₹35,000 he was being asked to pay buys comfort, and the real fuel saving attached to it is about ₹30 every 500 km — a couple of cups of chai.

That gap between the brochure and the bank account is what this guide is about. Not whether CNG automatics exist — they do, and [the full story of why they took a decade to arrive](/blog/why-cng-cars-are-not-automatic/) is worth reading separately. This is the decision in front of you: two pedals or three, and what each one actually costs over five years.

**The short answer:** Buy the CNG AMT if you drive in dense traffic at least 1,000 km a month and you are keeping the car beyond four years. Buy the CNG manual if you drive mostly on open roads, if the car is a fleet vehicle with multiple drivers, or if ₹35,000–₹40,000 is real money to you — because the automatic's fuel advantage is a test-cycle artefact, and it will not repay the premium in your lifetime of ownership. Either way, the fuel choice (CNG over petrol) saves far more money than the gearbox choice ever will.

## What you are actually choosing between

Almost every "automatic" in the CNG market is an AMT — an automated manual transmission. Under the floor there is still a normal manual gearbox with a clutch. A computer works the clutch and the shifter using actuators, and it decides when to shift based on speed, throttle and load.

There is no torque converter, no belt-and-pulley CVT, no dual-clutch pack. That matters because the three-pedal and two-pedal versions of the same CNG car are mechanically much closer than the price gap suggests — and because the failure modes you should worry about are manual-gearbox failure modes: clutch wear, actuator wear, and software that has been calibrated badly.

| | CNG manual | CNG AMT |
|---|---|---|
| Clutch | You operate it | Computer operates it |
| Gearbox internals | Manual | Same manual |
| Fuel efficiency in the test cycle | Lower | Slightly higher |
| Fuel efficiency in real traffic | Similar | Similar, sometimes worse in jams |
| Wear item | Clutch (100,000+ km in skilled hands) | Clutch (60,000–100,000 km, traffic-dependent) |
| Repair complexity | Low; any garage | Needs a diagnostic tool for clutch relearn |
| Price premium | — | ₹35,000–₹40,000 |

If you want the engineering explanation for why the industry settled on the AMT rather than a CVT or a torque converter, that is in [the companion piece](/blog/why-cng-cars-are-not-automatic/). The short version: an AMT keeps the manual's efficiency and cost base, which is exactly what a car sold on running cost needs.

## The mileage paradox: why the automatic claims more km/kg

Here is the number that sold my friend on the AMT: 35.34 km/kg against 32.85 for the manual. A 7.6% efficiency gain from a gearbox that adds weight and loses a little power to hydraulic actuation. It sounds too good to be true, and it mostly is.

| Model | CNG manual (claimed) | CNG AMT (claimed) | Claimed gain |
|---|---|---|---|
| Swift | 32.85 km/kg | 35.34 km/kg | +2.49 km/kg (+7.6%) |
| Dzire | 33.73 km/kg | 36.47 km/kg | +2.74 km/kg (+8.1%) |
| Baleno | 33.61 km/kg | 34.47 km/kg | +0.86 km/kg (+2.6%) |

The gain is real in the laboratory because the laboratory is a fixed script. The MIDC test cycle is a repeating pattern of acceleration ramps, steady cruises and decelerations. A machine that shifts at exactly the same point on every ramp, every time, will beat a human who shifts at 2,100 rpm on one run and 2,600 on the next. The AMT also upshifts earlier under light load, which is precisely the condition the cycle spends most of its time in.

On a real road, that advantage shrinks to almost nothing. An attentive manual driver in flowing traffic can match an AMT; in heavy traffic the AMT's clutch slip at crawling speed can make it slightly thirstier. My working assumption for any purchase decision is that CNG manual and CNG AMT return the same real-world figure: about 27 km/kg for a Swift-sized car in mixed driving.

Which means the fuel saving from the ₹35,000 premium looks like this:

| Real-world AMT advantage | Cost per km at ₹86.98/kg | Saving per km | Distance to recover ₹35,000 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 km/kg (realistic) | ₹3.22 | ₹0.00 | Never |
| +0.25 km/kg | ₹3.19 | ₹0.03 | ~11.8 lakh km |
| +0.5 km/kg | ₹3.16 | ₹0.06 | ~5.9 lakh km |
| +1.0 km/kg | ₹3.11 | ₹0.11 | ~3.0 lakh km |
| +2.0 km/kg | ₹3.00 | ₹0.22 | ~1.6 lakh km |
| +2.49 km/kg (Maruti's claim) | ₹2.95 | ₹0.27 | ~1.3 lakh km |

Read the last row again. Even if you believe the full claimed advantage survives contact with Indian traffic, you would need about 1.3 lakh kilometres to break even on ₹35,000. At 1,200 km a month, that is nine years. The automatic is a comfort purchase, and the sooner you accept that, the better the decision you will make.

### What this means for the "automatic saves fuel" claim

It is technically true and practically irrelevant. If you want the automatic for your left leg, buy it and enjoy it. Just do not let a brochure number talk you into a ₹35,000 upgrade you did not want, and do not let it talk you out of one you did.

## The running cost that does pay back

The gearbox premium is noise. The fuel choice is the signal. Using Delhi prices of ₹102.12 a litre for petrol and ₹86.98 a kg for CNG, and realistic rather than claimed efficiency:

| Configuration | Realistic efficiency | Cost per km |
|---|---|---|
| Petrol manual | 18 km/litre | ₹5.67 |
| Petrol AMT | 17.5 km/litre | ₹5.84 |
| CNG manual | 27 km/kg | ₹3.22 |
| CNG AMT | 27 km/kg | ₹3.22 |

The gap between CNG and petrol is about ₹2.60 a kilometre — roughly forty-five times larger than the fuel advantage the automatic claims over the manual, which works out to about six paise. That is the only comparison that should drive the fuel decision.

| Monthly running | CNG AMT fuel bill | Petrol AMT fuel bill | Monthly saving |
|---|---|---|---|
| 800 km | ₹2,577 | ₹4,668 | ₹2,091 |
| 1,200 km | ₹3,866 | ₹7,002 | ₹3,136 |
| 1,500 km | ₹4,833 | ₹8,753 | ₹3,920 |
| 2,000 km | ₹6,443 | ₹11,671 | ₹5,228 |

At 1,200 km a month, the ₹82,500 CNG premium over a petrol AMT is recovered in about 26 months on fuel alone — or roughly 39 months once you load in the extra CNG servicing, insurance and the clutch reserve. Both are inside a normal ownership period. That is a genuine payback, and it is the reason to buy CNG.

## Six ownership differences the brochure skips

The purchase decision takes an afternoon. These differences take five years to show up.

**1. The clutch is still a wear item, and it wears faster.**
An AMT clutch slips every time the car pulls away, creeps, or holds itself on a slope. In dense traffic, expect replacement somewhere between 60,000 and 100,000 km — earlier if you habitually hold the car on the throttle at signals. Budget ₹15,000–₹25,000 including the software relearn. A careful manual driver can exceed 100,000 km on a clutch.

**2. Creep is clutch slip, not torque-converter creep.**
A torque-converter automatic will crawl forward at idle all day without wearing anything. An AMT cannot. Its creep function deliberately slips the clutch, which generates heat and wear. In a long jam, do not rely on idle creep for minutes at a time; use the accelerator in short pulses, and shift to neutral for long waits.

**3. Hill starts need a different technique.**
With less torque in gas mode and a computer feathering the clutch, a steep incline with a full load is the AMT's weakest moment. Use the handbrake to hold the car, then release it as the clutch bites. If the car has hill-hold assist, confirm it works in CNG mode during the test drive, not just on petrol.

**4. Parking manoeuvres are jerkier.**
At 3–5 km/h, an AMT is caught between engaging and disengaging. Three-point turns and tight basement ramps need a lighter, more progressive throttle than a manual. Some people adapt in a week; some never do. Try it before you buy.

**5. Your service options shrink.**
Clutch replacement and actuator calibration usually need a manufacturer-specific diagnostic tool. A good local garage can service the engine and the CNG kit; it may not be able to relearn the gearbox. If the nearest authorised workshop is 60 km away, that is a real ownership cost.

**6. Resale cuts both ways.**
An urban buyer shopping for a commuter will pay more for the AMT. A fleet buyer, a driving school or a high-mileage operator will prefer the manual for durability and repair cost. Factory CNG automatics are still a young category, so there is no deep price history; full service records and a documented clutch replacement matter more than usual.

## The 20-minute test drive that settles it

Most test drives are a loop around the dealer's block: 2 km of smooth road, a U-turn, and a sales pitch. That route cannot tell you whether the AMT calibration is good. This one can.

1. **Start cold, on gas.** Ask the salesman to switch to CNG before you set off. Listen for injector ticking and watch the idle for hunting.
2. **Pull away at 30% throttle.** Count the pause between first and second, and second and third. A good AMT pauses for a heartbeat; a bad one pauses long enough for you to notice the car decelerating.
3. **Crawl at 8–10 km/h for 200 metres.** Find traffic or a speed-breaker queue. This is where clutch judder and shunt show up. If the car shudders, you will feel it every day.
4. **Stop on an incline, then pull away with the AC on.** Add passengers if you can. This is the worst-case load case for a low-torque gas engine.
5. **Reverse into a tight space.** Low-speed reversibility is where AMTs feel most clumsy.
6. **Do a 40–80 km/h kickdown.** Note how long the gearbox takes to decide, and whether it downshifts one gear or two.
7. **Use manual mode on a flyover.** Confirm the paddles or lever respond immediately and hold the gear.
8. **Switch fuel while moving.** The changeover should be imperceptible. A shunt here points to calibration problems.
9. **Check the boot with your own luggage.** Take the cabin bag out of your current car and put it in. Also confirm the spare wheel is present and accessible.
10. **Ask for the variant code in writing.** "Swift VXi CNG AGS" is a variant; "CNG automatic" is a conversation.

If the dealer will not allow a route with a slope and a traffic queue, that is information too.

## How to read a CNG brochure without being misled

Four specs cause most of the confusion.

**The tank is quoted in litres of water capacity, not kilograms of gas.** A "55-litre" CNG tank holds roughly 8.3–9.3 kg of gas at 200 bar. That is why the fuel gauge is in kg and why the range maths never matches the tank size.

**Claimed range is optimistic by design.** Take the tank's usable gas mass and multiply by a realistic figure, not the ARAI one:

| Figure | Calculation | Range |
|---|---|---|
| Claimed (Swift CNG AMT) | 8.5 kg × 35.34 km/kg | ~300 km |
| Realistic mixed use | 8.5 kg × 27 km/kg | ~230 km |
| Usable between fills (you rarely run dry) | 7.5–8 kg × 27 km/kg | ~200–215 km |

Plan on 200–250 km between fills. That is fine for a city commuter and irritating on a highway run, where the detour to a station can eat the saving.

**Kerb weight and GVW tell you what the tank cost you.** A CNG conversion adds roughly 60–90 kg, plus the automatic's actuators. Check the gross vehicle weight and the seating capacity on the registration certificate — some CNG variants are homologated as four-seaters because the tank's mass counts against payload.

**"AMT" and "AGS" are the same thing.** Maruti calls it AGS, Tata calls it AMT, and some brochures say "automatic". All three mean an automated manual. If a listing says "CVT" next to a CNG badge, treat it as a data-entry error until proven otherwise.

## Five things dealers get wrong

- **"The automatic has no clutch."** It has a clutch, and you will pay to replace it.
- **"The automatic gives more mileage, so it pays for itself."** It gives more on the test cycle. The payback is over a lakh kilometres.
- **"The 55-litre tank gives you 55 litres of gas."** It gives you 8–9 kg of gas and about 230 real-world kilometres.
- **"CNG servicing is the same as petrol."** Filters, plugs, valve checks, hydrotest and insurance loading all come on top. [The CNG running-cost comparison](/blog/petrol-vs-diesel-vs-cng-running-costs/) puts numbers on it.
- **"The on-road price is final."** CNG attracts a higher own-damage insurance loading, and the AMT's higher ex-showroom price can nudge registration tax up in states that slab by price. Ask for the break-up.

## Who should buy which

| Your situation | Buy this | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1,200+ km/month, mostly city, station within 15 minutes | **CNG AMT** | Comfort and running cost both land in your favour |
| 1,200+ km/month, mostly highway | **CNG manual** | The AMT's advantage is in traffic, not at cruise |
| 600–800 km/month, city | **Petrol AMT** | CNG will not repay its premium inside your ownership period |
| Fleet, ride-hailing, multiple drivers | **CNG manual** | Cheaper to repair, more durable, easier to hand over |
| Learner or nervous driver | **CNG AMT** | Removing the clutch removes most of the stress |
| Hilly route with a full load | **Test drive first** | Use manual mode; check the gradient with passengers aboard |
| Keeping the car 8+ years | **CNG AMT** if you drive enough | The fuel saving compounds; budget for one clutch |
| Changing cars every 3 years | **CNG manual** or petrol | The premium will not amortise, and AMT resale is unproven |

## The dealership checklist

1. Get the variant code and match it to the official price list.
2. Confirm the gearbox type in writing — AMT, not CVT, not torque converter.
3. Ask which tank layout the variant uses: boot-mounted single, twin cylinders, or underbody.
4. Confirm the spare wheel is included and reachable with a full boot.
5. Check the seating capacity on the RC and the GVW on the sticker.
6. Declare CNG to your insurer on day one and confirm the own-damage loading.
7. Read the extended warranty terms for the actuator, the clutch and the CNG kit — clutches are usually excluded as wear items.
8. Note the cylinder's manufacture date and set a reminder for the three-year hydrotest.
9. Find the nearest workshop that can perform an AMT clutch relearn, and ask what it costs.
10. Do the full PDI, including a CNG leak test at the nozzle, before you take delivery.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is the CNG AMT worth ₹35,000–₹40,000 over the CNG manual?

As a comfort feature, yes, if you sit in traffic daily and are keeping the car for years. As a fuel-saving investment, no. At realistic efficiency the two gearboxes return almost the same km/kg, so the premium is recovered over a lakh kilometres at best. Buy it because your left leg deserves it, not because the brochure promised a payback.

### Does the CNG automatic really deliver better mileage than the manual?

On the ARAI test cycle, yes — Maruti quotes 35.34 km/kg for the Swift CNG AMT against 32.85 for the manual, and 36.47 against 33.73 for the Dzire. In real traffic the difference is small enough to ignore, and in stop-go jams the AMT can be slightly worse because of clutch slip. Treat the claimed gain as a tie-breaker, not a reason.

### Which is better in heavy traffic: CNG AMT or CNG manual?

The AMT, without question. Stop-go traffic is the one condition where the two-pedal car transforms the experience, and it is also where the CNG saving is largest in rupee terms, because both fuels burn more per kilometre in a jam and the price gap per unit stays the same. Just learn to use the accelerator in short pulses rather than relying on idle creep.

### How much does an AMT clutch replacement cost?

Typically ₹15,000–₹25,000 including labour and the software relearn, depending on the model and the workshop. Expect it between 60,000 and 100,000 km in dense traffic, and earlier if you hold the car on the throttle at signals instead of using the handbrake.

### Can I drive a CNG AMT on hills with a full load?

Yes, but it is the weakest use case. Gas mode gives up roughly 15% of the engine's power, and the AMT may hunt between gears on a gradient. Use manual mode, drop a gear before the climb rather than during it, and test the exact scenario with passengers before you buy.

### Is a CNG automatic reliable long-term?

A factory CNG AMT is a validated product, and the two wear items — the clutch and the actuator — are known quantities with known costs. The bigger risk is a workshop that cannot calibrate it. Keep the servicing at an authorised centre, and the car should behave like any other automatic.

### CNG automatic or petrol automatic for 1,000 km a month?

Run the arithmetic rather than the instinct. At 1,000 km a month, a CNG AMT saves about ₹2,600 a month in fuel, so the ₹80,000-odd CNG premium takes around 31 months to recover before maintenance and closer to 47 months after it. If you keep the car five years, CNG wins; if you change every three, the petrol automatic is simpler and probably cheaper overall.

## Final thoughts

The most useful thing I can tell you about this decision is that it is two decisions wearing one hat.

The first is fuel: petrol or CNG. That one is worth ₹2.60 a kilometre, and it repays its premium in two to four years for most city drivers. The second is gearbox: manual or AMT. That one is worth a few paise a kilometre and a great deal of comfort, and it never repays anything.

Get the order right. Decide the fuel on arithmetic, then decide the gearbox on how you actually drive — and be honest about whether you sit in traffic or sail down a highway. The salesman's 35.34 will still be on the brochure either way; it just should not be the number that decides.
